# Positioning & Messaging — Passive Coder

Last updated: 2026-09-02. Owner: Wali.

This is the foundation document. The funnel SOP, reactivation SOP, and pricing
doc all assume the positioning below. If this changes, those change too.

---

## 1. The core problem with how we sell today

We market the CMS the way we marketed WordPress builds. Same channels, same
pitch, same close. But the economics are opposite:

| | WordPress build | Passive Coder CMS |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | One-time, ~$300–800 | Recurring, $40–160/mo |
| Success = | Delivery | Month 13 renewal |
| Cost of a bad-fit client | Absorbed once | Bleeds every month |
| What must be sold | A deliverable | An ongoing relationship |

Selling recurring revenue with one-time tactics wins the signup and loses the
renewal. Our own numbers show it: 44 tenants, 39 stuck in `onboarded`, and
until this week nobody was ever asked to pay.

**The fix is not more traffic. It is selling the right thing to the right
person, and asking for money.**

## 2. Who actually buys

Ranked by evidence, not by hope.

### Primary — Bangladeshi expat business owner in Middle East / Singapore / Malaysia

- Runs a real trading, construction, services, or retail business abroad
- Has money, has customers, has WhatsApp — does **not** have a credible web presence
- Often burned before: paid a freelancer who vanished, or got a one-page site that does nothing
- Buys in Bangla, thinks in BDT, pays in USD/SGD/AED
- **Their real fear is not price. It is being cheated again.**

This is our sharpest segment and the one the `/website-for-bangladeshi-businesses`
page already speaks to correctly.

### Secondary — local service business in SG / BD

Trades, salons, clinics, contractors. Found through referral and existing
client networks. Slower, warmer, higher retention.

### Not our buyer (say no)

- Anyone shopping purely on price against a $50 Fiverr one-pager
- Startups wanting custom product development
- Anyone who wants to own the code and self-host

Naming who we turn away is part of the position. A CMS that is for everyone
retains nobody.

## 3. The actual position

> **Passive Coder is the business website you can prove is real — run by a
> company you can prove is real.**

Not "we build websites." Not "cheap and fast." The wedge is **legitimacy on
both sides of the transaction**:

**Their legitimacy.** A one-page site says *someone made me a page*. A real
business site with services, portfolio, reviews, booking, and invoicing says
*this is an operating company*. That difference decides whether a customer in
Dubai or Singapore trusts them enough to send money.

**Our legitimacy.** This is the part competitors cannot copy, and it is already
on the BD landing page:

- Registered trade licence — a real, government-registered company
- A physical office, not a Facebook page
- Corporate bank account — payment goes to a company, not a personal bKash
- A real development team, not one freelancer
- Verified CEO identity on Facebook and LinkedIn
- 12,000+ YouTube subscribers, 200+ public videos showing the actual work
- **You get your dashboard before you pay**

That last point is the strongest sentence we own. It inverts the entire fear.

## 4. Why not a cheap one-page site — the argument

This is the Arman/Tarek video's argument. Keep it, it is correct. Sharpen it
to this shape:

**The cheap site is not badly built. It is built for the wrong job.**

A one-pager answers "do you exist?" A business website has to answer:

1. Can I trust you with money? (credentials, real address, reviews, portfolio)
2. What exactly do you sell, and what does it cost? (services, packages)
3. How do I start, right now? (booking, quote request, WhatsApp)
4. Will you still be here in a year? (updated content, live blog, ongoing work)

A one-pager answers question 1 badly and questions 2–4 not at all. So the
buyer's money is not wasted — it is spent on something that was never going to
generate a customer.

**Never argue that cheap sites are low quality.** Argue they are the wrong
tool. It is a stronger claim and it does not insult anyone's past decision.

## 5. Language

### Use

- "A website that brings you customers" — not "a beautiful website"
- "You get your dashboard before you pay"
- "Registered company, real office, corporate account"
- "Your site stays yours" — addresses the vanishing-freelancer fear
- Plain BDT/SGD/AED numbers, all-in, no asterisks

### Avoid

- "Cheap", "affordable", "budget" — we are not competing on price and cannot win there
- "Just", "simply", "easy" — implies the buyer's problem is trivial
- Emojis in any marketing or client-facing copy
- "SEO optimised", "responsive", "modern" — every competitor says these; they carry zero information
- Any number we cannot defend if asked (see §6)

### Bangla vs English

BD-expat segment: Bangla first. Their trust is built in Bangla, and the
existing landing page proves it works. SG/international: English.
Never machine-translate one into the other — rewrite.

## 6. Claims discipline

Every public number must survive a customer checking it.

- **"500+ websites built"** — all-time across all our work, including WordPress.
  Correct as written. Do **not** rephrase it as "live businesses on the
  platform" (we have 44) or "active websites today". This was wrong on the
  homepage until 2026-09-02.
- **"12 countries"** — countries served, all-time.
- Never state an uptime SLA number we do not measure.
- Never state a customer count that includes test/demo tenants.

A prospect who catches one inflated number discards every other claim on the
page. Given that our whole position is legitimacy, an inflated stat is not a
small marketing sin — it is a direct hit on the thing we are selling.

---

## 7. The one-line test

If someone asks "what do you do?", the answer is:

> "We build and run business websites for expat business owners — with a real
> company behind it, and you see your dashboard before you pay a taka."

If a piece of marketing does not ladder up to that sentence, cut it.
